## Guest Lecturer

**Natural Climate Solutions**, Dept. of Geography, UCLA (2024, 2025, 2026)

A survey of the many pathways available to address the climate crisis beyond ending fossil fuel use. The course examines nature-based solutions that draw down atmospheric carbon dioxide through both reduced emissions and increased carbon uptake, via ecosystem protection, management, and restoration. Students explore the interplay of natural and human dimensions of the carbon cycle, with a central focus on how the global and regional carbon cycle is changing and what that means for mitigation strategy.

## Teaching Assistant

**Sustainable Energy Systems**, Dept. of Earth System Science, UC Irvine (2020, 2021)

Addresses how modern energy services can be delivered sustainably, and the challenges and barriers standing in the way. The course covers major environmental issues tied to energy systems, including climate change, air pollution, and resource demands, alongside the technological and policy pathways toward a lower-carbon energy future.

**Sustainable Food and Water Systems**, Dept. of Earth System Science, UC Irvine (2019)

Explores the biophysical underpinnings of food production, the history of agricultural development, and the environmental issues facing modern agricultural systems, including water management, climate change, and land use. The course connects food security challenges to the broader Earth system processes that sustain them.

**Introduction to the Cryosphere: Climate Change and the Cryosphere**, Dept. of Earth System Science, UC Irvine (2020, 2021)

Introduces the fundamental science governing the cryosphere and its interactions with the broader climate system. The course also covers the significant economic, sociological, and political consequences of the ongoing, anthropogenically driven melting of the cryosphere, connecting physical science to real-world impacts.

**Fundamentals of GIS for Environmental Science**, Dept. of Earth System Science, UC Irvine (2017)

An introduction to Geographic Information Systems (GIS), covering the fundamentals of cartography, creating and editing GIS data, linking spatial and tabular data, georeferencing, map projections, geospatial analysis, and spatial statistics. Students build and apply GIS models using examples drawn from hydrology, ecology, and geology.

**Introduction to Earth System Science**, Dept. of Earth System Science, UC Irvine (2016)

Covers the origin and evolution of the Earth, its atmosphere, and its oceans, approached through the lens of biogeochemical cycles, energy use, and human impacts on the Earth system. Serves as a foundational course connecting Earth's physical history to present-day environmental change.
